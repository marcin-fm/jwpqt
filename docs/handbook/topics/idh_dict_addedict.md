# Adding Entries to EDICT

JWPqt's User Dictionary lets you collect locally useful EDICT-style entries, but it does not submit them or modify bundled EDICT. The packaged EDICT payload is an unmodified external dataset with its own notice and noncommercial or permission conditions. In a data-free installation, a user-supplied EDICT resource is likewise external data: retain its notice, do not relabel it as GPL, and confirm that redistribution is permitted before sharing it.

If you prepare entries for an external EDICT maintainer or another dictionary project, start by checking that resource's current contribution process and newest data. The legacy rules remain good data hygiene: use verbs in plain form, begin the English verb sense with `to `, and add `(vi)` or `(vt)` to the first translation when needed. For nouns, use the suitable grammatical marker such as `(an)`, `(a-no)`, or `(vs)` rather than appending Japanese particles or `suru`. Describe suffix and prefix use with `(suf)` and `(pref)`, rather than hyphens in the Japanese spelling.

Do not add an article to an English noun unless it distinguishes a sense. Do not guess kanji. Ensure kana spelling is sound, especially ambiguous romanized long vowels and `zu`, and do not add forms mechanically derivable from common suffixes when the root already covers them. Do not use raw `/`, `[` or `]` where they conflict with EDICT record syntax. Most importantly, do not submit definitions copied directly from a copyrighted source without permission; a contribution must be lawful as well as useful.

Create and review records through [Adding or Editing Entries](help:IDH_DICT_USEREDIT). A local entry is not necessarily contribution-ready merely because the native editor accepts it: imported legacy-compatible records may retain broader syntax, and local meanings may be tailored to personal use. Save produces only the configured user dictionary; it does not rebuild an indexed EDICT file, alter `dict.cfg`, or contact a network service.

To make an entry available in searches, keep the User resource configured and checked in **Tools > Manage Dictionaries**. The user resource is mixed/unindexed and cannot be removed from the registry, although its location can be changed. Its lack of an index makes ordinary editing practical; its source order and user-list order are preserved until you explicitly sort. See [Dictionaries](help:IDH_DICT_DICTIONARIES) for registry scope and [User Dictionary](help:IDH_DICT_USERDICT) for the atomic Save and import boundary.
